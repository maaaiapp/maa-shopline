TECHNICAL VALIDATION CHECKLIST

SHOPLINE technical validation checklist

The 26-state MVP is locked as documented in the Implementation Blueprint. This is the technical discovery agenda that determines what of it can actually be built against the real SHOPLINE platform for the Live Customer pilot. It assumes no API capability that SHOPLINE has not confirmed. Thirteen items, prioritised. Four are P0 and block the pilot.

P0 — blocks the Live Customer pilot · P1 — affects MVP implementation · P2 — Phase 2 or future.
Owner names below are the roles the question needs. Actual named contacts must be filled in at kickoff.

The four P0 items · nothing ships without these

P0-1Exact Admin API scopes and available dataDefines the ceiling of every feature. Without it, Health Check cannot be scored.
P0-2Product review accessibilityDetermines whether Voice of Customer is a real flagship or runs on behavioural signal only.
P0-3Programmatically executable campaign channelsDetermines whether the Campaign Planner can act or only advise.
P0-4Campaign metric read-back and attribution methodologyDetermines whether the loop closes. Without it there is no measured result to show.

P0 · Blocks the Live Customer pilot

P0-1BLUEPRINT ITEM 01

Exactly which Admin API scopes exist, what data does each expose, and what is the granularity and history depth of orders, customers, products and marketing objects?

Why
Every downstream feature is bounded by this answer. The onboarding scope screen (03) explains each scope in terms of the capability it unlocks, and the Health Check (09) scores dimensions that must degrade honestly when a scope is missing. We cannot write either without the real scope list.
Owner
SHOPLINE Developer Platform / Open Platform API team, with Partner Engineering as escalation.
Needs
Admin API reference for orders, customers, products, marketing; the full OAuth scope enumeration with per-scope field-level coverage; rate-limit policy; a sandbox store with representative data.
Affects
03, 04, 06, 08, 09, 11, 13, 26, 30 — and by extension all four flagships.
Fallback
None acceptable. If scopes cannot be enumerated, the scope-to-feature mapping on 03 is rewritten to whatever is confirmed and Health Check dimensions without data are shipped unscored, not estimated.
Decide
Which scopes MAA requests at install, and which Health Check dimensions are scoreable in the pilot.
P0-2BLUEPRINT ITEM 02 · FLAGSHIP 02

Are product reviews, review text, ratings and order notes readable through the API — and if so, at what volume, with what PII, and under what retention terms?

Why
Voice of Customer Intelligence is one of the four flagship experiences. Its entire proposition is themes clustered from what customers actually wrote, with visible source counts. Behavioural signal alone cannot produce a verbatim.
Owner
SHOPLINE Open Platform API team for availability; Legal / Data Protection for verbatim retention and PII handling.
Needs
Review or comment API documentation (or written confirmation of its absence); PII classification of review fields; permitted storage and processing terms for customer-authored text.
Affects
14 primarily; evidence folds on 08, 17, 28; opportunity generation quality on 16.
Fallback
MVP VoC runs on behavioural signal plus merchant-supplied verbatims, and the screen states its source count and its limits explicitly. External review and social ingestion moves from Phase 2 into the MVP critical path if the flagship is to hold.
Decide
Whether the pilot's VoC is SHOPLINE-native, merchant-supplied, or requires an external provider brought forward — and whether the flagship claim changes accordingly.
P0-3BLUEPRINT ITEM 05 · FLAGSHIP 04

Which marketing channels can be executed programmatically through SHOPLINE — email, Instagram, WhatsApp, SMS — and for each: create, schedule, target an audience, and cancel?

Why
The prototype commits to a specific, honest capability matrix on screen 25: email and Instagram schedule natively, WhatsApp exports, SMS is unconnected. That matrix is a product promise. If it is wrong in either direction, the Campaign Planner either over-claims or under-delivers.
Owner
SHOPLINE Marketing / Campaigns product team, plus Partner Engineering for the channel integration surface.
Needs
Marketing or campaign API documentation per channel; audience and segment API; scheduling semantics including timezone handling; idempotency guarantees; per-channel regional availability.
Affects
23, 24, 25 — the Campaign Planner flagship end to end.
Fallback
The Planner allocates budget only to validated channels. Everything else is presented as export or unavailable with the reason named. The Planner must never allocate to a channel it cannot execute.
Decide
The exact capability matrix shipped on 25, and whether the pilot campaign is single-channel.
P0-4BLUEPRINT ITEM 07 · CLOSES THE LOOP

What campaign delivery metrics are readable back, and what order-to-campaign attribution does SHOPLINE support — real attribution, discount-code linkage, UTM, or nothing beyond a time window?

Why
The pilot requirement is a measured result. Screen 26 compares actual against forecast and derives the lesson that generates the next opportunity. Without read-back the loop does not close and the product reduces to advice.
Owner
SHOPLINE Analytics / Reporting team, plus the Marketing product team for campaign-level metrics.
Needs
Campaign metrics API — sends, opens, clicks per channel; order attribution fields or discount-code and UTM linkage; attribution window definition; reporting latency.
Affects
26 primarily; 09 re-scoring after a campaign; 16 next-opportunity generation; all forecast confidence on 23.
Fallback
Time-window correlation, labelled on screen as correlation and not attribution, with the window stated. Discount-code linkage is the preferred fallback if available, since it is defensible.
Decide
The MVP attribution model, the exact wording of the attribution disclosure on 26, and the pilot's success measure.

P1 · Affects MVP implementation

P1-1BLUEPRINT ITEM 03

What are the bulk historical read limits, pagination behaviour and rate limits for orders and customers?

Why
Intelligence initialisation (04) targets a 12-month baseline. Whether that is an interactive wait or a background job is decided by this answer.
Owner
SHOPLINE Open Platform API team.
Needs
Rate-limit policy, pagination and cursor semantics, bulk or export endpoint documentation, guidance for large stores.
Affects
04, 09, 13, 26.
Fallback
Shorter baseline window; init becomes a resumable server-side job with a partial-baseline state on 06 and 09.
Decide
Baseline window length and whether init blocks first use.
P1-2BLUEPRINT ITEM 04

Which webhook topics exist for order, customer, product and app-lifecycle events, and what are the delivery and retry guarantees?

Why
Alerts (10) and the freshness stamp on the briefing (06) depend on it. It also determines whether uninstall is detected proactively or on next call.
Owner
SHOPLINE Open Platform API team.
Needs
Webhook topic list, registration API, signature verification, retry and ordering guarantees.
Affects
06, 10, 30.
Fallback
Sync-cadence alerts only, each labelled with its evaluation time. Polling schedule set against rate limits.
Decide
Sync cadence, and what MAA promises about alert latency.
P1-3BLUEPRINT ITEM 06

Can MAA write customer segments or audiences back into SHOPLINE, and can a campaign target one?

Why
Campaign preview (24) resolves an audience count before approval, and execution (25) needs a targetable audience. Without write-back the merchant must build the segment by hand.
Owner
SHOPLINE Marketing / Customers product team.
Needs
Segment or audience API with create, resolve-count and campaign-targeting semantics.
Affects
24, 25.
Fallback
Audiences are described precisely, not created. The merchant applies them manually and 24 says so.
Decide
Whether the pilot campaign targets a MAA-defined segment or a merchant-built one.
P1-4BLUEPRINT ITEM 08

Can a scheduled campaign be cancelled or modified after MAA schedules it, and is scheduling idempotent?

Why
Execution (25) must not offer a cancel action it cannot perform, and a token expiring mid-write must be safely retryable.
Owner
SHOPLINE Marketing / Campaigns product team.
Needs
Campaign update and delete semantics; idempotency key support on write endpoints.
Affects
25.
Fallback
Cancellation is not offered. Partial-success states are reported per channel with no rollback claimed.
Decide
Whether 25 shows a cancel affordance at all.
P1-5BLUEPRINT ITEM 09

What is the install-initiated launch contract from the SHOPLINE app store, and how does OAuth2 combine with the App Installation Token in practice?

Why
A merchant who installs from the SHOPLINE side must land somewhere coherent. The app cannot assume every connection starts at screen 01.
Owner
SHOPLINE Developer Center / App Store team.
Needs
App installation and OAuth flow documentation, redirect and callback contract, token lifetime and refresh behaviour, mobile app listing requirements.
Affects
01, 02, 30.
Fallback
In-app connect only; app-store install routes to a web handoff.
Decide
Onboarding entry points for the pilot.
P1-6BLUEPRINT ITEM 12

How are guest-checkout orders represented, and can a customer identity be resolved across orders without an account?

Why
Cohorts, retention and churn risk on 13 are only as good as identity resolution. A store that is mostly guest checkout produces misleading cohorts unless coverage is stated.
Owner
SHOPLINE Open Platform API team.
Needs
Order and customer object documentation covering guest orders and any identity linkage fields.
Affects
13, 14, 24, 26.
Fallback
Cohorts reported with an explicit coverage percentage; segments suppressed below a minimum n.
Decide
Whether the pilot store's identity coverage is sufficient for cohort claims.
P1-7BLUEPRINT ITEM 10

Can one MAA account be linked to multiple stores, and how does store switching work?

Why
MVP is single store by decision, but the token and account model must not foreclose multi-store later.
Owner
SHOPLINE Developer Center / Partner Engineering.
Needs
Multi-store token model and merchant account structure.
Affects
02, 29, 30 — architecture only in MVP.
Fallback
Single store per MAA account, with the data model built to allow more.
Decide
Account-to-store cardinality in the data model.

P2 · Phase 2 or future

P2-1BLUEPRINT ITEM 11

Is billing handled through the SHOPLINE app purchase flow or directly, and how is referral-fee-bearing revenue reported?

Why
Required by the revenue-tracking terms of the agreement, and it determines whether subscription management can be in-app at all.
Owner
SHOPLINE Partnerships / Business Development, with Finance for reporting format.
Needs
App billing API or confirmation of out-of-app billing; referral attribution and reporting format.
Affects
33 (Phase 2); commercial obligations from day one.
Fallback
Out-of-app billing; referral attribution tracked server-side by MAA.
Decide
Billing route before the pilot converts to a paid customer.
P2-2BLUEPRINT ITEM 13

Does SHOPLINE expose any category or peer benchmark data MAA may use?

Why
Health Check peer comparison and Market Intelligence both depend on a benchmark source. Neither is claimed in MVP.
Owner
SHOPLINE Analytics / Data team; Partnerships for data-use terms.
Needs
Benchmark or industry-data availability and permitted use.
Affects
09 peer comparison; 12 and 15 in Phase 2.
Fallback
Score against the store's own history only, with no peer claim. External provider for Phase 2.
Decide
Whether Market and Competitor Intelligence need a commercial data provider.

Discovery sequence

  1. Session one — Open Platform API team. P0-1, P0-2, P1-1, P1-2, P1-6. Outcome: the confirmed data surface, and whether Voice of Customer is SHOPLINE-native.
  2. Session two — Marketing / Campaigns product team. P0-3, P1-3, P1-4. Outcome: the real capability matrix for screen 25.
  3. Session three — Analytics team. P0-4, P2-2. Outcome: the attribution model and the pilot's success measure.
  4. Session four — Developer Center and Partnerships. P1-5, P1-7, P2-1. Outcome: install flow, account model, billing route.
  5. Then and only then: a sandbox store with representative data, and a written capability baseline that implementation is built against.

The four P0 answers are what convert this specification into a buildable pilot. Until they are written down, any implementation against the Campaign Planner, Voice of Customer, Health Check scoring or results attribution is speculative.
